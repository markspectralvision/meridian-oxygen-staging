General Questions

What is an on-site oxygen supply system?

An on-site oxygen supply system is an oxygen concentrator plant installed in your building that produces medical oxygen from room air and feeds your medical gas pipeline. At Meridian Oxygen we specify it as a second or third supply source that runs beside your existing bulk or cylinder supply, with its own storage, purity monitoring and alarms.

Is a Meridian system a portable oxygen generator?

No. A portable oxygen generator serves one patient at the bedside or at home. A Meridian system is a central supply source connected to the hospital pipeline, sized from 30 to 180 liters per minute, with a receiver, a purity analyzer and master alarm outputs.

Does an on-site system replace bulk liquid oxygen?

No, and we do not sell it that way. The fire code requires a second independent source in every configuration, and the equipment class is described for use as a supply where bulk oxygen is not readily available and as a backup source. Meridian systems are specified alongside your existing supply.

Who buys these systems?

Hospitals, surgery centers, long-term care groups, emergency medical services stations and clinics that want a hospital oxygen concentrator supply source under their own roof, often in regions far from an air separation plant or with a history of delivery disruption. The purchase runs through the value analysis committee, and our packet is written for that committee.

What is the first step?

Request the value analysis packet. It contains the specification, the redundancy configuration, the sizing worksheet, the service contract terms and the disclosure of limits. An engineer follows up within one business day to answer questions about oxygen demand and siting.

Does Meridian Oxygen make any claims about regulatory clearance?

Only when confirmed in writing, and your hospital safety protocols deserve nothing less. Until then, our documents describe what the equipment produces and how it is monitored, and we state the regulatory context plainly. We would rather you hear that from us than discover it in committee.

The Technology

How does a PSA oxygen generator work?

Pressure swing adsorption compresses filtered room air through a bed of zeolite. The zeolite holds nitrogen under pressure and lets oxygen pass. When pressure drops the bed releases nitrogen to vent while a second bed takes over, so oxygen production is continuous. The product gas is stored in a receiver, analyzed for purity and regulated to pipeline pressure.

What is Oxygen 93?

Oxygen 93 Percent is a United States Pharmacopeia monograph for oxygen produced from air by the molecular sieve process. It contains not less than 90.0 and not more than 96.0 percent oxygen by volume, with the remainder mainly argon and nitrogen. It is a separate article from Oxygen USP.

How is oxygen purity monitored?

A continuous oxygen sensor reads product gas concentration and drives local and master alarms when concentration falls below 91 percent. The reading is displayed on the cabinet and reported to the service team. A sample port near the analyzer allows independent verification.

What does the fire code require of an oxygen concentrator supply?

NFPA 99 has addressed oxygen concentrator central supply since its 2018 edition. Requirements include product gas meeting Oxygen 93 USP or Oxygen USP, a 0.1 micron filter at the outlet, a check valve, an outlet valve with manual and automatic actuation, a vent valve for testing, a sample port, purity alarms, and a second supply source such as a cylinder header.

Is a Meridian system an industrial oxygen generator?

The physics are the same as an industrial oxygen generator: pressure swing adsorption over zeolite. The medical version adds continuous purity analysis, master alarm outputs, oxygen-service filtration at the outlet, a second supply source, and a specification written to Oxygen 93 USP. Those additions are what a hospital pipeline requires.

Why does oxygen purity drift?

Purity drift is the first sign of a service need: a clogged intake filter, a dryer past its dew point, a leaking valve seat or a warm plant room. Meridian systems are specified to run above the nominal 93 percent so drift appears on a trend line before it reaches the alarm threshold.

Are there clinical limits to Oxygen 93?

Two. Some air-oxygen blenders specify oxygen of at least 99 percent, and the achievable inspired fraction from a 93 percent source tops out near 0.93. In closed or minimal-flow anaesthesia circuits argon can accumulate, and published guidance is fresh gas flow at or above twice oxygen consumption with periodic venting. We disclose both in every packet.

What does the oxygen pipeline feed downstream of the supply source?

Every outlet on your pipeline: ventilators, the oxygen flow meter at each wall outlet, oxygen masks and cannulas used for oxygen therapy, oxygen humidifiers and other oxygen therapy equipment, every oxygen flowmeter and oxygen saturation monitor on the unit, and the ICU equipment that provides respiratory support and respiratory care. Respiratory therapy equipment keeps its oxygen saturation monitoring routine. Respiratory support equipment and oxygen delivery systems downstream of the outlet, and the oxygen saturation targets your clinicians set, do not change because the supply source changed. An oxygen tank or cylinder header remains connected as the second source.

Why On-Site

Why is delivered oxygen a regional market?

Bulk liquid oxygen ships economically only 100 to 250 miles from the air separation unit that made it. The Federal Trade Commission drew seventeen regional markets in the United States on that basis and found each highly concentrated. Your supply competes inside one circle.

What is on a delivered oxygen invoice besides the gas?

Typically tank rental, delivery charges, fuel surcharges, medical compliance fees and facility fees, on top of minimum purchase requirements and price escalation clauses in multi-year contracts. The largest supplier describes the leased tank and the requirement contracts in its own annual report.

How much has the price of oxygen changed?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics producer price index for industrial oxygen rose from 309.0 in 2019 to 716.0 in 2025, an increase of 131.7 percent. It is a public federal series.

When does an on-site oxygen supply earn its place?

Where delivery is genuinely hard: islands, mountain regions, facilities far from an air separation plant, and any facility that saw its emergency oxygen supply stretched during a surge. A published Hawaii installation tied a containerized 600 liter per minute unit into its emergency oxygen supply connection after the 2021 surge.

Would on-site generation have stopped the 2021 oxygen shortages?

No, and we will not tell you it would have. The documented 2021 failures were frozen vaporizers, undersized pipe and a shortage of drivers with hazardous materials endorsements. A second source helps with delivery dependence. Undersized pipe restricts 93 percent oxygen exactly as it restricts 99 percent.

What happens to an oxygen plant that sits idle?

It degrades. Published field studies report zeolite damage after roughly two months unused, and replacement can cost a large share of the plant's value. That is why Meridian sells the service contract with the system: the contract funds the duty cycle that keeps the plant healthy.

Systems

What flow rates are available?

Six models from 30 to 180 liters per minute. Larger demand is met by pairing cabinets in a multi-source arrangement, which also serves the redundancy requirement.

How is an oxygen supply system sized?

By peak calculated demand, not average draw. Each source in a multi-source arrangement must carry the full demand on its own. Published guidance puts a moderate-acuity patient on high-flow support near 16.5 liters per minute, and a simple facility estimate adds a base allowance per bed to a per-outlet allowance for intensive care and operating rooms. The packet includes the worksheet.

What pressure does the system store and deliver?

Product gas is stored at 165 PSI in an ASME receiver and regulated down to pipeline pressure through the required filter and check valve.

What does the plant room need?

Space for the cabinet, the receiver and the air pretreatment train, coordinated with hospital ventilation, three-phase power on the essential electrical system, heat rejection that your hospital ventilation systems must carry, and a location where the air side of the concentrator is not in the same enclosure as cylinder manifolds or bulk headers. Feed air quality matters: the specification calls for 40 to 80 degrees Fahrenheit and a dry intake.

Can the system be installed outside?

Yes. A containerized unit with the generator, receiver, compressor and vacuum in one enclosure sits on a pad and ties into the emergency oxygen supply connection with minimal piping change.

Who maintains the system?

A service team trained on the equipment as installed, under a contract that covers filter changes, dryer service, purity verification, alarm testing and response times. Remote monitoring reports purity and pressure so visits are scheduled from the trend line, not the alarm.
