Delivery dependence

Why is delivered oxygen a regional market?

Because liquid oxygen is heavy, cold, and cheap per unit, it cannot travel far from the cryogenic oxygen plant that made it before freight costs more than the gas. Liquid oxygen storage on your site is the end of that trip, not the start of your independence.

A 100 to 250 mile circle

The Federal Trade Commission found that bulk liquid oxygen ships economically only that far from an air separation unit, and drew seventeen separate regional markets across the country, each one highly concentrated. Your oxygen supply competes inside one circle.

The tank is leased, not owned

The largest supplier states in its own annual report that the bulk vessel on your site is owned and maintained by the supplier and leased to you, under multi-year requirement contracts with minimum purchase and price escalation provisions. Oxygen storage you rent is a line item that never ends.

The price moved 131.7 percent

The Bureau of Labor Statistics producer price index for industrial oxygen rose from 309.0 in 2019 to 716.0 in 2025. That is a public federal series, not a vendor estimate. It measures the exposure a second source hedges.

The cylinder is where the money is

Awarded state contracts price bulk oxygen per cubic foot at a small fraction of the price of the same oxygen in an E cylinder. If your facility runs on an oxygen cylinder program, or pays cylinder rental, delivery, and hazmat fees on top of a bulk contract, that is the spend a second source addresses first.

Resilience

When does an on-site source earn its place?

When the nearest air separation plant is a ferry ride or a mountain pass away. When a surge puts oxygen demand in the intensive care unit beyond the plan. When a vaporizer ices in a cold snap and the bulk system slows. A published hospital installation in Hawaii tied a containerized 600 liter per minute unit into its emergency oxygen supply connection after the 2021 surge, on an island with no oxygen plant of its own.

That is the honest use case for emergency response and everyday resilience: a second or third supply source that your pipeline can draw on, sited where delivery is hardest, running every day so it is ready on the day. An idle plant degrades. A running system does not. That is why Meridian sells the service contract with the machine.
